** What is deadline backpressure, and should we build it?

*What it is.*  Today a task is either a commitment (due today or earlier)
or a candidate scored by urgency.  A six-hour task due Friday is a
candidate on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday -- it may win on urgency,
but nothing /forces/ it in until Friday, when it becomes a commitment
that cannot fit.  Backpressure is the rule that the deadline pushes work
backwards into the days before it:

- *start-by* = deadline minus the days the calibrated effort needs at the
  free capacity of those days.  A task past its start-by is committed,
  not scored.
- *slicing* = the packer plans /part/ of a task on a day (a =:slice= of
  minutes), so the six hours land as 2+2+2 across Tuesday to Thursday.

Soft deadlines get none of this.  It was ranked last (G11, size L) on the
plan's own rule: only once the packer is trusted and calibration has real
data, because slicing changes what a plan /is/ -- every invariant in the
core assumes whole tasks.

*Recommendation.*  Build it in two halves, neither of them as a separate
feature.  Start-by is cheap and belongs in the day packer (Part 3, one
function and four tests).  Slicing is a property of the *horizon
scheduler* (Part 3): once the planner simulates several days at once,
"fit this before Friday" is just what it does, and slices fall out of
placing a task across the days it needs.  Doing slicing inside the
single-day packer would be building the multi-day scheduler without
admitting it.

** Does the planner respond to tags?  To effort?

*Effort: yes, it is the unit of everything.*  The packer works in minutes:
each task's =:Effort:= (calibrated by the measured estimate-versus-clock
ratio for its category) is subtracted from the day's usable minutes as it
is admitted; a task with no estimate costs =org-queue-default-effort= (30)
and is drawn as guessed.  The =quick= term rewards efforts at or under 15
minutes.  Effort is why a day fills, not just how many tasks it holds.

*Tags: only two ways, and neither is capacity.*  Tags are harvested and
used for (1) the context/energy /availability/ filter --
=org-queue-contexts= '("@call") drops tasks that carry a different
context tag, untagged tasks always pass -- and (2) nothing else.  The
=glut= penalty is per /category/ (the file), not per tag, and it is a
score nudge, not a budget.  "Five hours for work, one for reading, thirty
minutes for housekeeping" does not exist.  Part 1 designs it.

The sentence in TASK-TRACKING.org ("the planner reacts to four things")
is about what makes a task /committed or excluded/; it should say so, and
now does.

** Does it update any metadata?

No.  The only file it writes is =.data/org/queue-history.el= (the IDs it
planned, for carry-over scoring).  "Pulled in" is a label on a report.
This was a deliberate choice -- a planner that writes SCHEDULED stamps is
distrusted the first time it guesses wrong -- and Part 4 keeps the
argument while adding the write: nothing lands without review, and what
lands stays distinguishable from what a person wrote.

** Habits?

Nothing.  =org-habit= is not loaded; no entry carries =:STYLE: habit=; the
fixture's repeaters ("Water the plants +3d", "Take the bins out +1w",
"Weekly review .+1w") are harvested as ordinary tasks -- scored
candidates on most days, commitments on the day their repeater lands.
The day's fixed routine (lift, bike, clean) lives in prose in
=~/kb/notes/schedule.org= and the planner has never read it, so today's
"5:00 capacity" is a guess that silently includes or excludes the lift
depending on how you read it.  Part 2 designs habits as *reservations*.

* Part 1 — Buckets: capacity by kind

** The idea

A day is not one pool of minutes.  It is a few pools with names: focus
work, reading, housekeeping, calls.  Each has a budget, and a task
belongs to one of them.  The packer fills each pool from the tasks that
belong to it.  Overflow in one pool is reported as overflow /of that
pool/; an empty pool is reported as unfilled, not silently handed to
another.

** Definition

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-queue-buckets
      '((work         :minutes ((1 . 300) (2 . 300) (3 . 300) (4 . 300) (5 . 240))
                      :match (:category ("work" "emacs")))
        (reading      :minutes 60
                      :match (:tags ("reading" "@read")))
        (housekeeping :minutes 30
                      :match (:tags ("housekeeping" "@errand" "home")))
        (default      :minutes 60)))
#+end_src

- =:minutes= is an integer (every day) or a per-weekday alist like
  =org-queue-capacity=.  A weekday absent from the alist has 0 -- the
  bucket does not exist that day, and its tasks are cut with reason
  =bucket-closed=.
- =:match= is a plist of any of =:category=, =:tags= (any of), =:pred= (a
  function of the task plist).  The *first* bucket whose match holds
  wins, in list order.  A task no bucket claims goes to =default=.
- =:spill t= (optional) lets a bucket's unspent minutes be used by
  =default= at the end of packing.  Off by default: a reserved half hour
  for housekeeping that nothing claims is a finding ("nothing to do in
  the house today"), not free work time.
- Slack (=org-queue-slack-fraction=) is taken per bucket.
- When =org-queue-buckets= is nil, the planner behaves exactly as today:
  =org-queue-capacity= /is/ the default bucket.  Nothing existing changes.

*Semantics chosen: partition, not ceilings.*  A bucket is a reservation
(a floor: the packer will fill it even when higher-scoring work exists
elsewhere) and a limit (a ceiling: no more than its minutes).  The
alternative -- one pool with per-tag caps -- answers "no more than 30
minutes of housekeeping" but not "make sure I read for an hour", and the
second is the one the routine actually wants.  =:spill= recovers the
ceiling-only behaviour per bucket for anyone who wants it.

** Packing with buckets

1. Classify every eligible task into a bucket.
2. Admit commitments into their buckets.  A commitment overflows only
   its own bucket; the report says "work OVERCOMMITTED by 0:40" and still
   packs reading and housekeeping.  Today's all-or-nothing OVERCOMMITTED
   becomes per bucket, and the alarm banner appears when /any/ bucket
   overflows.
3. Fill each bucket from its candidates, highest score first, against
   its own usable minutes.  WIP limit stays global.  The glut penalty
   stays as it is (category, within a bucket).
4. Spill pass for buckets that allow it.
5. Pull-forward pass, per bucket.

Cut reasons gain =bucket-full= (with the bucket's name) and
=bucket-closed=.  The invariant -- every task in exactly one of planned,
cut, dropped, deferred -- holds unchanged; a task also carries
=:queue-bucket=.

** Report

The header line becomes one line per bucket:

#+begin_example
Tuesday 8 September 2026
   work          3:40 of 4:00     reading   0:48 of 0:48     housekeeping  0:00 of 0:24  (nothing claimed)
#+end_example

A =b= key groups the planned lines by bucket instead of by reason; the
bucket name is also available as a column (Part 4 of TASK-TRACKING's
column table gains =B=).

** Where the table lives, eventually

First cut: a =setq= in =modules/org/org-queue.el=, like capacity.  Once G6
(the routine table in =schedule.org=) is read by code, the buckets are
derived from its rows -- "Focus block 1 (4h)" is the work bucket, "Free
time / admin" is housekeeping -- and the =setq= goes away.  Do not block
buckets on G6; the derivation is a later, small step.

** Tests (batch, no Org)

- a task tagged housekeeping never consumes work minutes;
- a reading bucket with 60 minutes and 90 minutes of reading candidates

- a bucket with no candidates reports 0 planned and =default= does not

- a weekday absent from a bucket's alist cuts its tasks with

* Part 2 — Habits as reservations

** What a habit is, to the planner

A habit is time you have already decided to spend, on a repeating
pattern, that is not a task to be finished.  Lifting an hour a day
except Sundays; guitar; the bike; laundry; dishes.  It does not compete
with the backlog.  It is *subtracted from the day before packing
begins*, like an appointment with a repeater, and then shown so the day
adds up.

That is all Phase 1 does.  Strength, streak-free scoring, the two-miss
alert and the graph are Phase 2 and follow G4's design.

** Entry shape

In a =(todo) routine.org= (real corpus and fixture alike):

#+begin_example
* TODO Lift weights  :body:
SCHEDULED: <2026-09-12 Sat .+1d>
:PROPERTIES:
:STYLE:     habit
:Effort:    1:00
:HABIT_DAYS: Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat
:END:

* TODO Guitar practice  :music:
SCHEDULED: <2026-09-12 Sat .+1d>
:PROPERTIES:
:STYLE:     habit
:Effort:    0:30
:END:
#+end_example

- =:STYLE: habit= is the discriminator, as org-habit expects.  A repeater
  /without/ it stays a repeating *task* ("Water the plants +3d" still
  queues as work).  You decide per entry.
- =:HABIT_DAYS:= is a weekday set; absent means every day.  This is the
  "except Sundays".
- =:Effort:= is the reservation.  A habit with no effort reserves
  =org-queue-default-effort= and is drawn as guessed, like a task.
- Ticking DONE rolls the repeater forward, as org does today; the
  reservation for that day is unchanged (it was spent).

** Planner treatment

- Harvest keeps habits (they are TODO headings) but tags the plist
  =:habit t=.
- The filter drops them from candidates with reason =habit= -- never
  planned, never cut, never scored.
- Before packing, the day's habits (those whose =:HABIT_DAYS:= includes
  the day) are summed and subtracted from capacity: with buckets, from
  the bucket they match (the lift matches a =body= bucket if there is
  one; otherwise =default=).  If the routine alone exceeds the day, that
  is reported as such -- "routine 5:30 exceeds capacity 5:00" -- before
  any task is considered.
- The report gains a *Routine* section above Committed, drawn dim, and
  the header shows the arithmetic: =capacity 5:00, routine 1:30, usable
  2:48=.

** Interaction with the routine table

=schedule.org= already lists the lift, the bike and the clean as timed
rows.  When G6 lands, those rows can /generate/ =(todo) routine.org= (the
G6 text says exactly this), so the file above is the machine-readable
form and the table stays the prose.  Until then the file is written by
hand; four entries is not a burden.

** Phase 2 (later, G4)

Load =org-habit= for the agenda graph and consistency window; strength
via =org-habit-stats= or a pure core with Loop's 13-day half-life; the
two-miss alert through =zetta-notify=; =K= to hide habits in the agenda.
None of that changes the planner; Phase 1 is complete on its own.

** Tests

- a habit is never in planned or cut; it is dropped with reason =habit=;
- a habit with =HABIT_DAYS= excluding Sunday reserves nothing on Sunday;
- the reservation reduces usable minutes before commitments are admitted;
- a habit matching a bucket reduces that bucket alone;
- a routine that exceeds capacity is reported and the packer plans nothing;
- a repeater without =STYLE=habit= is still a task.

** As built [2026-09-12]

Phase 1 as designed.  Slack is taken /after/ the routine -- =(capacity -
routine) x (1 - slack)= -- because routine is fixed time and slack is for
the work that is not.  The fixture gained =(todo) routine.org= with five
habits (lift, bike, clean, guitar, laundry) and =HABIT_DAYS=.  The
generator's self-check skips them.  Six tests.

* Part 3 — Horizons: planning more than one day (read-only)

** The simulator

=org-queue-core-horizon (tasks from to &optional capacity-fn)= runs the
existing day packer once per day across a range and returns a list of
day plans plus the remainder.  The pool evolves:

- a task planned on day N is removed from the pool for N+1 (the
  simulation assumes it gets done; the report shows this assumption);
- a task deferred to day D becomes eligible on D;
- appointments and habits apply to the day they fall on, repeaters
  rolled forward;
- carry-over is not simulated (it describes the past, not the future).

Scales are just ranges: rest-of-today, day, N days, week, fortnight,
month, and *backlog* (run until the pool is empty or a ceiling of 90
days, and report what never fit).  Sub-day is Part 4's last step.

** Backpressure lives here

Two additions to the core, in this order:

1. *Start-by* (in the day packer, immediately usable): for a task with a
   hard deadline, =start-by= = the latest day on which the remaining
   calibrated effort still fits into the free minutes of the days up to
   the deadline (free = usable minus commitments, from the simulator; or,
   in the single-day command, from =org-queue-capacity= alone as a first
   approximation).  On or after start-by the task is a commitment.  The
   note reads "start by <date>" or "past start-by".
2. *Slices* (in the simulator only): when the day packer is asked for a
   day and a committed task's remaining effort exceeds the day's free
   minutes, it admits a slice -- =:queue-minutes= smaller than the
   effort, =:slice t= -- and the remainder stays in the pool for the next
   day.  A task is sliced only when it is a commitment via start-by or
   deadline; candidates are never split.  Each day's invariant holds;
   the horizon's per-task summary shows placed versus total.

Soft deadlines get neither.  This is G11 in full, without a separate
feature.

** Display

=org-queue-horizon= (=,-o-h=; the agenda's =h= is under =,-o-v=, so no clash)
draws one section per day, collapsed to a single line each:

#+begin_example
Tue 15   4:00 of 4:00   work 3:40  reading 0:20     Q4 roadmap (slice 2 of 3), …
Wed 16   3:10 of 4:00   …
Thu 17   OVERCOMMITTED by 1:20   …

Never fits: 4 tasks, 11:30   TAB to see why.
#+end_example

TAB on a day expands to the full day report with the same columns and
keys as =*org-queue*=.  It is read-only.  Its point is to show what the
proposal in Part 4 would do before asking anything.

** As built [2026-09-12]

Two deviations, both decided by a test that would not pass any other way:

1. *The deadline day counts.*  Start-by walks back from the deadline
   /inclusive/, because an Org deadline is inclusive and the day view
   already calls work on that day "due today".  So six hours due Friday
   at two free hours a day is Wednesday to Friday, not Tuesday to
   Thursday, and Tuesday is free.  If "deliver the day before" is the
   rule you want, that is a one-line change in =org-queue-core-start-by=.
2. *In the simulation every commitment is sliced to what fits*, including
   one due today.  The single-day packer is unchanged (admits whole, says
   OVERCOMMITTED); the simulator instead reports the shortfall under
   =:missed=.  Appointments are the exception: a meeting has a time of day
   and is admitted whole.

Also: the free-minutes walk excludes the task asking the question (its
own deadline-day minutes are not "already booked"), and ignores machine
placements while proposing.  A repeating appointment is rolled forward
once by the harvest, so a daily standup appears on one day of a range;
known limit, noted in the file header.  Sixteen tests.

** Tests

- three days with two free hours each and a six-hour hard-deadline task
  due on the fourth: slices of 2+2+2, the task committed from day one;
- a task before its start-by is a candidate; the day after, a commitment;
- a slice never exceeds the day's free minutes and counts once against WIP;
- a soft deadline produces no slice and no start-by;
- a task planned on day N is absent from day N+1's pool;
- a backlog run terminates at the ceiling with the remainder reported.

* Part 4 — Write-back: propose, review, apply

** Principles, so the trust argument survives

1. *Nothing is written without a review.*  The proposal is a buffer; the
   apply is a keystroke on lines you have accepted.
2. *Human placements are facts.*  A SCHEDULED a person wrote is never
   moved by a proposal.  The proposal may /say/ "this would fit better on
   Thursday" as a finding, but it does not propose the move unless asked
   (=C-u=).
3. *Machine placements stay distinguishable.*  An accepted placement
   writes SCHEDULED /and/ a =:PLACED:= property with the apply stamp.  A
   later proposal may move a machine placement (it was a guess the
   machine made); the review shows it as a move, from → to.
4. *Deadlines are never proposed.*  They are facts the planner reads.  A
   task that cannot fit before its deadline is a finding in the proposal
   ("does not fit: 3:00 short by Friday"), not a suggested new date.
5. *States are never proposed.*  NEXT, HOLD, DONE are human decisions.
   The proposal places; it does not decide.
6. *Idempotent.*  Accept everything, run again, and the proposal is
   empty.  The proposal is the diff between the simulated horizon and
   what the files already say.
7. *Reversible.*  Every apply writes a log with the reverse actions;
   =org-queue-undo-apply= plays the last one back.

** The atom first: accept keys in =*org-queue*= (M0)

Before any bulk loop, the single-task version, because it is what the
loop applies many times and because it closes the gap already found in
TASK-TRACKING ("there is no gesture that makes a pull real"):

| key | on the task at point                          | writes                      |
|-----+-----------------------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| =S=   | schedule it today                             | SCHEDULED today, =:PLACED:=   |
| =L=   | schedule it later (prompts with =org-read-date=)| SCHEDULED that day, =:PLACED:= |
| =N=   | mark it NEXT                                  | state NEXT (org logs it)    |
| =H=   | hold it, with the note the state asks for     | state HOLD                  |
| =U=   | unschedule a machine placement                | removes SCHEDULED, =:PLACED:= |

Each redraws.  =N= and =H= write state, which contradicts principle 5 --
deliberately: these are /you/ pressing a key on one line, not a proposal.
The bulk loop never emits them.

** Actions

A proposal is a list of action plists, each self-describing:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(:action schedule   :id ID :to 20260915 :minutes 120 :bucket work :why "start-by passed; 2 of 3 slices")
(:action move       :id ID :from 20260914 :to 20260916 :why "Tuesday overcommitted by 0:40")
(:action unschedule :id ID :from 20260913 :why "no longer fits before its deadline; see finding")
(:finding :id ID :text "does not fit before 2026-09-18: short by 3:00")
#+end_src

=:id= is the entry's =:ID:=; an entry without one gets one at apply time
(the harvest already reads IDs, and =org-id= is configured).  Sub-day
actions (Part 4, last step) add =:start= and =:end= as minutes of the day
and write a timed SCHEDULED.

** Proposing

=org-queue-propose= (=,-o-p=) asks for a scope -- rest of today, today, N
days, week, fortnight, month, backlog -- runs the horizon simulator with
the current files as the starting state, then diffs:

- a task the simulation places on day D that has no SCHEDULED → =schedule=;
- a machine placement (=:PLACED:= present) on day A that the simulation
  puts on day B → =move=;
- a machine placement the simulation no longer places at all → =unschedule=;
- a human placement the simulation would have moved → a finding only;
- a task the simulation could not fit before its hard deadline → a finding.

*Stickiness.*  The simulator gives a task already placed on day D a
bonus for D, so a re-run does not churn last week's accepted plan for a
marginal score difference.  Moves are proposed only when D no longer
fits or a commitment displaced it.

*Rejection memory.*  A rejected line is recorded in
=.data/org/queue-rejections.el= as =(ID . TO-DATE)= with a stamp and is not
proposed again for =org-queue-rejection-days= (14).  This is Forster's
re-decision made cheap: you say no once, not every morning.  A different
target date is a new proposal.

** Reviewing

=*org-queue proposal*= is an org-queue-style buffer: sections per day,
one line per action, columns and toggles shared with =*org-queue*=, plus
=from → to= and the =why=.  Findings sit in their own section.

| key     | does                                                        |
|---------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| =a= / =r=   | accept / reject the line (or every line in the active region) |
| =A= / =R=   | accept / reject the whole day section                       |
| =C-c C-a= | accept everything                                           |
| =e=       | edit the target date for this line; it becomes a /human/ placement on apply |
| =RET= / =o= | jump to / show the entry                                    |
| =g=       | re-propose (keeps accept/reject marks by ID where the action is unchanged) |
| =C-c C-c= | apply the accepted lines                                    |
| =q=       | abandon; nothing written                                    |

Accepted lines are marked in the header face, rejected in the guess
face; the summary line counts both and the minutes that would land.

** Applying

1. Collect the accepted actions; group by file.
2. For each file, in its buffer, for each action: locate the entry by ID
   (never by position), then =org-schedule= with the date string (or
   =org-schedule '(4)= to remove), set or remove =:PLACED:=.  Org's own
   =org-log-reschedule= should be ='time= so the LOGBOOK carries the
   "Rescheduled from" line (an existing org mechanism; the fixture
   already emits such lines).
3. Only after every action in every buffer succeeded, save the buffers.
   Any error before that point: revert the modified buffers and report
   the failing action.  No partial apply.
4. Append the apply to =.data/org/queue-applies.el= with the reverse
   actions and a stamp.  =org-queue-undo-apply= reverses the most recent
   one through the same code path, so undo is itself logged.
5. Replan the =*org-queue*= buffer if it is open.

Never through =emacsclient --eval= with a prompt (see
[[file:TASK-TRACKING.org]] and the feedback memory); every prompt here is in
the interactive command only.

** What an accepted placement means afterwards

It is a SCHEDULED stamp, so the day packer treats it as it treats any
schedule: a commitment on its day, deferred before it, carry-over after
it.  The note shows =placed <date>= from the property.  That is the answer
to "what does pulled in mean": once accepted, it is on the calendar, and
=,-o-Q= reads it back like anything a person wrote.

** Sub-day: "the next four hours" (last step)

Requires blocks: G6's routine table read by code, so a day is a sequence
of blocks with a kind and, per Part 1, a bucket and a context/energy
restriction.  Then:

- "rest of today" = the blocks remaining after now; each block packed as
  a bucket of its own minutes;
- placements write a timed SCHEDULED (=<2026-09-12 Sat 14:00-15:00>=),
  which the timegrid draws as a block and lets you drag -- the writable
  grid becomes the manual edit surface, and the harvest reads the moved
  time back;
- a task larger than the block is sliced across blocks the same way
  slices work across days.

This is the piece with the most moving parts and the least evidence
behind it; it goes last, after day-level proposals have been lived with.

** Tests

- proposing twice with everything accepted in between yields an empty
  proposal;
- a human placement is never in a =move=, and appears as a finding when
  the simulation would have moved it;
- a rejected (ID . date) is absent from the next proposal within the
  window and present after it;
- apply with one failing action writes nothing and reports the action;
- undo after apply restores every SCHEDULED and removes every =:PLACED:=
  it wrote, and leaves human placements alone;
- an accepted placement is a commitment on its day in the next =,-o-Q=.

** As built [2026-09-12]

As designed, with these particulars:

- The atom is =org-queue-apply.el=: =org-queue-apply-actions= applies a
  list all-or-none, refuses a buffer with unsaved changes, reverts touched
  buffers on error, saves, and logs =:actions= and =:reverse= to
  =.data/org/queue-applies.el=.  =org-queue-undo-apply= (=,-o-u=) plays the
  reverse back and is itself logged, so a second undo redoes.
- =:PLACED:= carries an inactive stamp; the harvest reads it as =:placed=.
- The proposal additionally *skips what a date already fixes*: an
  appointment on its day and a task on its deadline day are not given a
  SCHEDULED they would only repeat.  A start-by commitment is.
- Rejections are =(KEY DATE DAY-RECORDED)= in =.data/org/queue-rejections.el=,
  KEY being the ID or (file . point).
- =e= on a line re-dates it and marks it accepted; on apply it is written
  /without/ the stamp, so it is yours from then on.
- Keys: =,-o-p= propose, =,-o-P= horizon (=h=/=H= are org-metaleft), =,-o-u=
  undo.  In the plan buffer =S L N H U=.  Eight apply tests, five proposal
  tests.

Sub-day (the last step) is not built.

* Part 6 — Open questions, yours

- *Partition or ceiling?*  Part 1 chose reservations with optional spill.
  If most days you would rather the packer just take the best work
  wherever it comes from, the default should be =:spill t=.
- *Should accepted placements be commitments?*  Part 4 says yes (they are
  SCHEDULED).  The alternative -- a softer "planned" that the next day's
  packer may quietly drop -- avoids overcommitting on the machine's
  guesses but reintroduces "what does this stamp mean".
- *Property name.*  =:PLACED:= is short; =:QUEUE_PLACED:= is unambiguous.
- *Rejection memory.*  Fourteen days, or until the entry changes
  (effort, deadline)?
- *Is a repeating task a habit?*  Part 2 says only with =STYLE=habit=.
  "Take the bins out +1w" is arguably a habit; you decide per entry, and
  the census can list repeaters without the property.
- *Is the timegrid the sub-day surface?*  It already writes timestamps
  on drag; Part 4 leans on it rather than building a second grid.
