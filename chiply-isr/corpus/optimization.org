#+TITLE: Trimming Latency from a Checkout Service

Our checkout endpoint had crept up to nearly two seconds at the ninety-ninth
percentile. A flame graph from the profiler pointed at a single culprit: each
request issued dozens of small database round-trips, one per line item. We
collapsed them into a single batched query and added a short-lived in-memory
cache for catalog lookups.

The tail dropped to under three hundred milliseconds. Users who had complained
that the page felt sluggish now saw it respond almost the instant they clicked.
The lesson that stuck with us: measure where the time actually goes before
touching a line of code, because the bottleneck is almost never where intuition
insists it must be. Throughput climbed as a side effect once the queue stopped
backing up behind those serial round-trips.
