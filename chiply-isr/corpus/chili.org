#+TITLE: Making Peace with the Ghost Pepper

The burn is chemistry, not temperature. Capsaicin binds to the very receptors
that warn you about a flame, which is why a habanero can leave your tongue
convinced it is on fire while the morsel itself is stone cold. Water only
spreads the oil around; reach for milk or yogurt, whose fat actually lifts the
molecule away.

Scoville numbers climb fast, but tolerance climbs too. Build it gradually and
the same peppers that once flattened you start to register as a warm, almost
pleasant glow rather than an ordeal you regret halfway through the plate.
